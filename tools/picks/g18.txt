# Occurrence
572+573+574 Group 18 consists of elements: helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon, radon and oganesson. All these are gases and chemically unreactive. They form very few compounds, because of this they are termed as noble gases.
575+576 All these gases except radon and oganesson occur in the atmosphere. Their atmospheric abundance in dry air is ~ 1% by volume of which argon is the major constituent.
577+578 Helium and sometimes neon are found in minerals of radioactive origin e.g., pitchblende, monazite, cleveite. The main commercial source of helium is natural gas.
579 Xenon and radon are the rarest elements of the group.
580T Radon is obtained as a decay product of ^226Ra: ^226_88Ra → ^222_86Rn + ^4_2He
580T Oganesson has been synthetically produced by collision of ^249_98Cf atoms and ^48_20Ca ions: ^249_98Cf + ^48_20Ca → ^294_118Og + 3n
581+582 The elements present in Group 18 have their valence shell orbitals completely filled and, therefore, react with a few elements only under certain conditions. Therefore, they are now known as noble gases.
583+585 Oganesson has its symbol Og, atomic number 118, atomic mass 294 and electronic configuration [Rn] 5f^14 6d^10 7s^2 7p^6. Its half life is 0.7 milliseconds.
# Atomic and physical properties
590! All noble gases have general electronic configuration ns^2np^6 except helium which has 1s^2.
591 Many of the properties of noble gases including their inactive nature are ascribed to their closed shell structures.
592+593 Due to stable electronic configuration these gases exhibit very high ionisation enthalpy. However, it decreases down the group with increase in atomic size.
594 Atomic radii increase down the group with increase in atomic number.
595 Since noble gases have stable electronic configurations, they have no tendency to accept the electron and therefore, have large positive values of electron gain enthalpy.
589T Table 7.12: Ionisation enthalpy/kJ mol^-1 He 2372, Ne 2080, Ar 1520, Kr 1351, Xe 1170, Rn 1037. Electron gain enthalpy/kJ mol^-1 He 48, Ne 116, Ar 96, Kr 96, Xe 77, Rn 68. Atomic radius/pm He 120, Ne 160, Ar 190, Kr 200, Xe 220.
596+597+598 All the noble gases are monoatomic. They are colourless, odourless and tasteless. They are sparingly soluble in water.
599 They have very low melting and boiling points because the only type of interatomic interaction in these elements is weak dispersion forces.
600! Helium has the lowest boiling point (4.2 K) of any known substance.
601! It has an unusual property of diffusing through most commonly used laboratory materials such as rubber, glass or plastics.
# Chemical properties
601+602+603 In general, noble gases are least reactive. Their inertness to chemical reactivity is attributed to the following reasons: (i) The noble gases except helium (1s^2) have completely filled ns^2np^6 electronic configuration in their valence shell. (ii) They have high ionisation enthalpy and more positive electron gain enthalpy.
605+606 In March 1962, Neil Bartlett, then at the University of British Columbia, observed the reaction of a noble gas. First, he prepared a red compound which is formulated as O2^+PtF6^–.
607 He, then realised that the first ionisation enthalpy of molecular oxygen (1175 kJ mol^–1) was almost identical with that of xenon (1170 kJ mol^–1).
608 He made efforts to prepare same type of compound with Xe and was successful in preparing another red colour compound Xe^+PtF6^– by mixing PtF6 and xenon.
609 After this discovery, a number of xenon compounds mainly with most electronegative elements like fluorine and oxygen, have been synthesised.
610+611 The compounds of krypton are fewer. Only the difluoride (KrF2) has been studied in detail.
612+616! Compounds of radon have not been isolated but only identified (e.g., RnF2) by radiotracer technique.
617! No true compounds of Ar, Ne or He are yet known.
704 Radon is radioactive with very short half-life which makes the study of chemistry of radon difficult.
# Xenon fluorides
618 Xenon forms three binary fluorides, XeF2, XeF4 and XeF6 by the direct reaction of elements under appropriate experimental conditions.
619T Xe(g) + F2(g) → XeF2(s) (673 K, 1 bar; xenon in excess)
619T Xe(g) + 2F2(g) → XeF4(s) (873 K, 7 bar; 1:5 ratio)
619T Xe(g) + 3F2(g) → XeF6(s) (573 K, 60-70 bar; 1:20 ratio)
619 XeF6 can also be prepared by the interaction of XeF4 and O2F2 at 143K. XeF4 + O2F2 → XeF6 + O2
619 XeF2, XeF4 and XeF6 are colourless crystalline solids and sublime readily at 298 K. They are powerful fluorinating agents.
620+621 They are readily hydrolysed even by traces of water. For example, XeF2 is hydrolysed to give Xe, HF and O2. 2XeF2(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2Xe(g) + 4HF(aq) + O2(g)
621+622 The structures of the three xenon fluorides can be deduced from VSEPR. XeF2 and XeF4 have linear and square planar structures respectively.
623! XeF6 has seven electron pairs (6 bonding pairs and one lone pair) and would, thus, have a distorted octahedral structure as found experimentally in the gas phase.
624 Xenon fluorides react with fluoride ion acceptors to form cationic species and fluoride ion donors to form fluoroanions.
625 XeF2 + PF5 → [XeF]^+[PF6]^–; XeF4 + SbF5 → [XeF3]^+[SbF6]^–; XeF6 + MF → M^+[XeF7]^– (M = Na, K, Rb or Cs)
# Xenon-oxygen compounds
625 Hydrolysis of XeF4 and XeF6 with water gives XeO3. 6XeF4 + 12H2O → 4Xe + 2XeO3 + 24HF + 3O2; XeF6 + 3H2O → XeO3 + 6HF
625+626 Partial hydrolysis of XeF6 gives oxyfluorides, XeOF4 and XeO2F2. XeF6 + H2O → XeOF4 + 2HF; XeF6 + 2H2O → XeO2F2 + 4HF
630! No, the products of hydrolysis are XeOF4 and XeO2F2 where the oxidation states of all the elements remain the same as it was in the reacting state.
627 XeO3 is a colourless explosive solid and has a pyramidal molecular structure.
628 XeOF4 is a colourless volatile liquid and has a square pyramidal molecular structure.

# Uses
631+632+633 Helium is a non-inflammable and light gas. Hence, it is used in filling balloons for meteorological observations. It is also used in gas-cooled nuclear reactors.
634 Liquid helium (b.p. 4.2 K) finds use as cryogenic agent for carrying out various experiments at low temperatures.
635 It is used to produce and sustain powerful superconducting magnets which form an essential part of modern NMR spectrometers and Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) systems for clinical diagnosis.
636 It is used as a diluent for oxygen in modern diving apparatus because of its very low solubility in blood.
637+638 Neon is used in discharge tubes and fluorescent bulbs for advertisement display purposes. Neon bulbs are used in botanical gardens and in green houses.
639 Argon is used mainly to provide an inert atmosphere in high temperature metallurgical processes (arc welding of metals or alloys) and for filling electric bulbs.
640 It is also used in the laboratory for handling substances that are air-sensitive.
641+642 There are no significant uses of Xenon and Krypton. They are used in light bulbs designed for special purposes.
